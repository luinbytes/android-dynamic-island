# Activity lifecycle and interactions

[Specification index](../../REFERENCE_SPEC.md) · [iOS reference hub](01-ios-reference.md)

Source-backed interaction, start, update, concurrency and end rules. Each target-build result remains a native capture task.

- The iOS 27 user guide specifies the current interaction grammar: touch and hold to expand, swipe up on expanded content to collapse, tap an activity to switch when two are visible, and swipe across the Island to move among activities on iPhone 18 Pro. This supersedes older user-guide wording about swiping from the center to expand. [iOS 27 iPhone User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iph28f50d10d/27/ios/27).
- A 2024 Apple Support video also describes hiding and reopening Live Activities in the Island. Its description does not specify the gesture or establish that hiding behaves the same on iPhone 18 Pro Max/iOS 27, whose guide now assigns cross-Island swipes to moving among up to three activities. R5d records positive or negative current-device trials and keeps visibility separate from task lifecycle. [Apple Support video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=srHDQ_lqLtE), [hide/restore contract](../features/37-activity-hide-restore.md).
- A third-party ActivityKit Live Activity can stay active for at most eight hours, then leaves the Island immediately; it may remain on the Lock Screen for up to four more hours. An app can mark content stale earlier. Ending or dismissing the Lock Screen card is distinct from canceling the underlying task. These rules govern ActivityKit content, not necessarily every first-party system indicator. [ActivityKit lifecycle](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Apple limits a Live Activity's combined static and dynamic data to 4 KB, including data supplied in ActivityKit updates and pushes. Oversized image assets can also prevent start under presentation-specific limits; these are separate constraints. The Live Activity extension cannot fetch network or location updates itself, so the publisher app or ActivityKit push must supply changed task data. R1 and R6a record accepted source state, errors and visible frames separately for boundary trials; no exact iPhone 18 failure appearance follows from the documentation alone. [ActivityKit constraints](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Within one iOS app, ActivityKit's `relevanceScore` determines which of its Live Activities appears in the Island and their Lock Screen order; equal scores favor the one started first. Apple says an app with multiple activities and no competing app still shows one compact activity. These documented per-app rules do **not** explain iPhone 18's three visible positions across apps; capture score changes, ties and mixed publishers before encoding an Android arbiter. [ActivityKit relevance score](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/activitycontent/relevancescore), [ActivityKit presentation guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Apple's Dynamic Island design session shows an ongoing app experience moving into the Island when the person leaves the app. This is evidence for an app-to-Island handoff as a distinct motion path, but it predates iPhone 18 and does not provide that phone's trajectory, timing or a universal transition for every publisher. Capture entering and returning to the source app separately from a Live Activity's content update. [WWDC23 design session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10194/).
- People can disable a third-party app's Live Activities and separately allow frequent ActivityKit push updates. `areActivitiesEnabled` and `frequentPushesEnabled` are distinct, observable authorizations; the latter changes the publisher's permitted update frequency, not its basic start permission. A missing or late activity on the reference iPhone may therefore be a settings or transport result rather than a layout rule. Record both settings with each relevant capture and test changes while an activity is active. [Activity authorization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/activityauthorizationinfo), [push update budget](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ActivityKit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications).
- ActivityKit push payload timestamps let the system prefer the most recent update, and a push received after an activity ends is ignored. APNs priority 10 is immediate but budgeted; priority 5 is lower priority and outside that budget. Neither delivery priority nor `isActivityUpdateReduced` (which Apple documents for synchronization to remote devices such as Apple Watch) defines the iPhone Island's own animation frame rate. R22a separates transport receipt, accepted source state and visible frames. [Apple ActivityKit push guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ActivityKit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications), [remote-device update value](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/isactivityupdatereduced), [push ordering contract](../features/39-push-update-ordering.md).
- A third-party expanded Live Activity can contain buttons backed by `LiveActivityIntent`, so expanding is not always just a larger status display. The iOS 27 WWDC example updates the underlying task through an intent rather than opening the app. Capture enabled, pressed, pending, success, failure and dismissal behavior for a real interactive activity; the exact Island response is still unmeasured. [WWDC26 Live Activities essentials](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/223/).
- Apple also documents intent-backed **toggles** in expanded and Lock Screen Live Activities. A toggle can show an optimistic on/off change before its intent finishes; on a locked device, controls are inactive until authentication and unlock. R18a separates that feedback/commit/rollback path from a momentary button. Exact iPhone 18 Pro Max/iOS 27 visuals and lock behavior remain to be captured. [Apple interactive Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/adding-interactivity-to-widgets-and-live-activities), [toggle contract](../features/31-interactive-toggle.md).
- Apple distinguishes ordinary Live Activity content updates from alerting updates. Its HIG says alerts can light the screen, play the default notification sound and display the expanded Island; an ordinary update should not be treated as an alert solely because content changed. Measure alert duration, interruption priority and the actual sound/wake behavior under Focus, muted and locked states. [Live Activities HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [ActivityKit alert configuration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/alertconfiguration).
- ActivityKit's Lock Screen-style **Home Screen banner** example applies to an unlocked device **without** Dynamic Island support when an alert-configured update arrives. Do not add that banner as a second required iPhone 18 Pro Max surface. For R6, observe the Island expansion and any other actual notification/system presentation separately; the target build's timing and coexistence still need native capture. [Apple ActivityKit presentation guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- In StandBy, Apple describes a Live Activity's minimal presentation and a tap transition to the Lock Screen presentation scaled to 200%, with its background extended to the display. This is separate from the unlocked Island and the ordinary Lock Screen card; the iPhone 18 Pro Max result still needs native capture. [Live Activities HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [WWDC26 Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/223/).
- ActivityKit can schedule a Live Activity for a future start. Before that date its state is `pending`, and the scheduling API requires an alert configuration so the person knows when it begins. Scheduled activities count toward the device's simultaneous-activity limit. This is a separate trigger and lifecycle from an ordinary update or an already-active activity; the iPhone 18 Pro Max start animation, alert and interaction with visible activities still need capture. [Apple scheduling guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities), [pending state](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/activitystate/pending).
- ActivityKit also supports a **remote push-to-start** token: APNs can start a new Live Activity and wake its publisher app, then token-targeted or channel updates can update/end it. Broadcast pushes can update/end an activity subscribed to a channel but cannot start one. This is a distinct arrival path from an on-device scheduled start. Apple's documentation describes the transport, not the exact iPhone 18 Island entrance, delay or alert result, so capture those separately. [Apple ActivityKit push guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ActivityKit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications).
- A `LiveActivityIntent` is another start path: a person can invoke a publisher control, such as one in Control Center, and the system can run its intent and start the activity without displaying the publisher's app UI. Keep this user-initiated start distinct from remote push, a pending scheduled start and tapping an action inside an already-running activity. [Apple LiveActivityIntent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/liveactivityintent), [ActivityKit start guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Apple also recommends an App Shortcut that starts a Live Activity and names the iPhone Action button as a user-configurable invocation path. R22c measures that physical-button start separately from R22b's Control Center control; the documentation does not publish the target phone's Island entrance or duplicate-start behavior. [Apple Live Activities HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [Action-button shortcut guide](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/shortcuts/apdfea15680b/ios), [start contract](../features/29-action-button-start.md).
- An App Clip can start a Live Activity displayed in the Island without the full app installed, using a Live Activities-only widget extension. R22d isolates this no-full-app publisher path from ordinary installed-app starts. Apple's guide does not establish whether an active clip activity transfers to the full app after installation on iOS 27; observe that transition rather than assume continuity. [Apple App Clip Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appclip/offering-live-activities-with-your-app-clip), [App Clip contract](../features/35-app-clip-live-activity.md).
- Apple's Live Activities guidance also recommends showing several event updates by rotating the content **inside one activity**. This is a publisher-authored layout/update pattern, not evidence of extra activity slots or a system carousel. R5c measures one stable source ID through those changes and contrasts it with separate activities. [Apple Live Activities HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [rotation contract](../features/30-one-activity-rotating-content.md).
- `ActivityStyle.transient` starts an ActivityKit experience in the expanded Island presentation while the person is performing a task. Apple says it ends automatically when the phone locks, the presentation is collapsed, the person taps outside, or they leave the app. This differs from a standard activity that merely expands temporarily for an alert or long press; capture its appearance and each termination path separately. [Apple transient-activity guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities), [style API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/activity/request%28attributes%3Acontent%3Apushtype%3Astyle%3A%29).
- Apple's ActivityKit guide gives Music as a first-party transient example: while starting audio in the app, an expanded Island can offer to move playback to a nearby HomePod using AirPlay. This is a distinct route-control flow from ordinary Now Playing transport buttons and from a third-party transient fixture. Capture the actual iPhone 18 Pro Max entry, route choice, success/failure and teardown; the example does not specify its exact visual geometry. [Apple ActivityKit guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
