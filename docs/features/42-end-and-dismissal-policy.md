# Activity end and Lock Screen dismissal policy

[Specification index](../../REFERENCE_SPEC.md) · [R12 capture](../reference/02c-system-surfaces-capture.md) · [Android map](../verification/10-state-to-android-map.md)

ActivityKit distinguishes `active`, `stale`, `ended` and `dismissed`. An `ended` Live Activity cannot receive more content updates, leaves the Dynamic Island immediately, and may continue showing final content on the Lock Screen. `dismissed` means the activity is no longer visible after a person or the system removes it. The task that originally caused the activity can still continue after a person removes the card. These are presentation/source-state distinctions, not four interchangeable animation names. [Apple ActivityState](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/activitystate), [Apple lifecycle and dismissal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).

For R12c, use one controlled ActivityKit publisher and distinct run IDs. End with **final content** under `.immediate`, `.default` and `.after(date)` policies, choosing a short future date within Apple's four-hour limit. Log the exact end call and returned state, the first Island-free frame, any next-source promotion, Lock Screen final-content frame and last visible frame, `activityState` sequence, and whether tap, dismiss, unlock and a late update change either surface. Repeat while locked, unlocked, in StandBy and with a second activity. An eight-hour automatic end remains R12a and a user's Lock Screen removal remains R12b. Apple's documented default is retention for some time, with automatic removal by four hours; `.after` is bounded by four hours. Do not turn those bounds into an exact iPhone 18 display duration without a timed native run. [Apple ending and dismissal guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).

On Android, keep an app-owned task's authoritative `active/ended` state separate from the replica's Island presentation and any posted notification. At a confirmed end, invalidate active controls and remove the replica's Island slot; if the chosen app-owned contract calls for a completion summary, publish and time that summary separately under Android notification policy. `Notification.Builder.setTimeoutAfter()` cancels a notification after a duration; it does not prove a task ended or reproduce Apple's Lock Screen retention and chip-removal timing. A notification listener's removal callback supplies a reason such as user cancellation, app cancellation or timeout, but cannot infer a third-party task's lifecycle from that reason alone. Inspect a named publisher contract before showing final state or scheduling disappearance. [Android notification timeout](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/Notification.Builder#setTimeoutAfter(long)), [listener removal reasons](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/service/notification/NotificationListenerService#onNotificationRemoved(android.service.notification.StatusBarNotification,android.service.notification.NotificationListenerService.RankingMap,int)), [notification cancellation](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/NotificationManager#cancel(java.lang.String,int)).

The paired verdict needs separate timestamps and outcomes for Island exit, native Android chip exit, final Lock Screen content, dismissal, source-task completion and late-update rejection. An APK cannot own the Android keyguard's notification layout or guarantee Apple's retention policy; a SystemUI route still requires proof on the selected build. Keep visual and functional verdicts `unverified` until those surfaces and transitions are captured on both devices.
